What Drives Crime Rates Across New York Counties?

A concise summary of our county‑level regression analysis for 62 New York counties (2023), including model selection with AIC/BIC and a Box–Cox transformation, plus a link to the full PDF.

📄 Download the full report (PDF) Preview in browser

Abstract

We study county‑level variation in New York State’s 2023 index crime rate (ICR) using multiple linear regression. Among eight socioeconomic candidates and a NYC dummy, model selection (AIC/BIC, all‑subsets, stepwise) identifies median income (MI), bachelor’s degree or higher share (BoH), and log total population (log‑TP) as the most informative predictors. A Box–Cox transformation improves diagnostics (normality and homoskedasticity). The final model attains strong explanatory power for a social‑science application and yields intuitive signs for MI (negative) and population scale (positive), while BoH shows a positive association plausibly reflecting urban concentration effects.

Key findings

  • Final model (BIC): y = β₀ + β₁·MI + β₂·BoH + β₃·log(TP) + ε
  • Direction: MI ↓ with ICR; BoH ↑ with ICR; log‑TP ↑ with ICR.
  • Model quality: R² ≈ 0.63 (adj. R² ≈ 0.615) with sound residual diagnostics after Box–Cox.

Full dataset and methods (AIC/BIC, Cp, VIF, diagnostics) are detailed in the PDF.
